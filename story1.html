INTERNATIONAL SAR FRIENDSHIP

Gifts That Carry a Legacy

Sometimes the most meaningful gifts are not measured by their value, but by the stories they carry.

Every visitor who walks through our rescue station brings a unique story. Some of those stories stay with us long after the visit has ended.

Recently we welcomed Glenn Yates from Olympia, Washington, USA, whose family has been part of the Search and Rescue community for nearly fifty years.

Glenn's father helped establish the Thurston County Explorer Search and Rescue (ESAR) unit in 1977 and spent the next three decades serving in leadership positions at both the local and state level. His mother was also an active registered leader for many years.

Following the passing of both his parents, Glenn chose to bring part of their family's legacy to Iceland.

A Gift from One Rescue Family to Another

Among the donated items were navigation compasses, waterproof field notebooks and official Search and Rescue patches from Washington State.

These are far more than pieces of equipment. They represent decades of volunteer service, training, teamwork and compassion.

Every compass, notebook and patch tells the story of volunteers who dedicated countless hours to helping people they had never met.

A Shared Mission Across the Atlantic

Although thousands of kilometres separate Iceland and Washington State, the values that guide Search and Rescue volunteers are exactly the same.

  • Helping others without expecting anything in return.
  • Working as a team.
  • Continuous training and learning.
  • Serving the community.
  • Being ready when someone needs help.

Glenn also shared the history of the Explorer Search and Rescue programme, which introduced generations of young volunteers to Search and Rescue before many continued serving as adult leaders throughout their lives.

Thank You, Glenn

We are honoured that Glenn chose to entrust us with these meaningful items.

His parents' legacy now continues in another corner of the world.

Every time one of these items is used during training or an operation, it will remind us that the Search and Rescue family extends far beyond national borders.

"The Search and Rescue family extends far beyond national borders."

— Skagfirðingasveit Search and Rescue Team
