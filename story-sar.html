My dad helped establish the Thurston County Explorer Search and Rescue (ESAR) unit in 1977. He was active and held leadership positions at the local and state levels for the next 30 years, so Search and Rescue has great meaning for our family. The gifts we brought were among many items we are processing following his death in 2017 and mom's death in 2025. Mom was also a registered leader for many years. When we learned that our O.A.T. trip would include a Search and Rescue piece, I looked for items I thought you might be able to use. The compasses are self-explanatory. The waterproof notepads may come in handy. I understand that it can rain at times here in Iceland. The patches represent state and local participation. The Explorer program is an offshoot of the Boy Scouts of America, designed for youth ages 14-21. Although ESAR privatized when the Boy Scouts wouldn't recognize the units as an official part of their program, I believe due to liability and insurance concerns, all members must be registered in the scouting program. Many continue as adult leaders after reaching the age of 21. The first link is our local Thurston County website, an the second for the State of Washington WESAR site. https://www.tcwesar.com/about https://www.wesar.org/history-of-wesa Thank you again for taking the time to share your program with our groups. It was a memorable experience. Glenn Yates Olympia, WA USA
